Walk into your visa interview calm and prepared; know the purpose behind each question in advance.

Consular preparation

Typical questions you may be asked in a visa interview and what each one assesses.

The goal is to help you be prepared and consistent, not to give you ready-made answers.

01 / 08

Work Visa (Skilled)

12 questions

01

Why do you want to work in Germany?

What this question assesses / how to approach

This question assesses whether your intention to come is genuine and career-based. It helps to be ready to talk about your professional background and why you chose Germany; a sincere rationale consistent with your profession and job offer builds trust.

Note

Basing your answer on your own professional story rather than a memorised, formulaic reason is more convincing; the officer notices generic, rehearsed answers.

02

What is your job offer/position?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Assesses whether the position matches your qualification. Knowing your job description, duties and responsibilities in line with your contract is important.

Note

If there is a gap between the job title and what you will actually do, being able to explain it is helpful; your account should match the description in the contract.

03

What is the salary of your job offer?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Checks whether your salary meets the visa threshold and matches your contract. Knowing the gross figure in your contract is important; the figure you state should match your contract exactly, inconsistency raises doubt.

Note

It is important not to confuse gross and net salary; speaking in terms of the official gross figure in the contract is safest.

04

How did you find this job?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Assesses the authenticity of your job-search process. Being able to describe your application/interview steps concretely is helpful.

Note

Even if you found the job through an agency/consultant, you are expected to recount the process yourself; a vague answer like 'on the internet' leaves question marks.

05

What is your qualification, is it recognised in Germany?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Assesses whether your qualification fits the position and visa requirement. Knowing your diploma's recognition status (Anabin/recognition) is important; for academic degrees the job often need not be in exactly the same field as the diploma, and generally the suitability of the qualification level is assessed.

Note

Your diploma being listed in Anabin is not the same as a formal recognition decision; for regulated professions (health, etc.) recognition is mandatory, so not confusing the two matters.

06

Do you have prior work experience in this field?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Assesses your fit for the position. It helps to summarise your past experience (duration, duties); if none, to explain your fit via education/skills.

Note

The experience you describe should match your CV and references exactly; an exaggerated or document-inconsistent duration can be seen as a discrepancy.

07

What is your German or English level?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Assesses your language level for the work environment. Stating your actual level (and certificate if any) honestly, and any improvement plan, is helpful.

Note

Claiming a level above your certificate can be noticed on the spot, since the interview itself is spoken; your stated and actual level should match.

08

Where will you stay in Germany?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Assesses whether you have a settled plan after arrival. Knowing your accommodation arrangement (rental, temporary, employer support) helps; if not finalised, expressing a realistic plan is important.

Note

A firm rental contract is not required, but having no idea at all gives an impression of being unprepared; even a temporary, realistic arrangement is enough.

09

Which company is your employer, what do they do?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Assesses the authenticity of the offer and your knowledge. Having basic information about your employer's name, sector and business builds trust.

Note

Not knowing your employer's sector or what they do can suggest the offer is only on paper; not mixing up basic facts matters.

10

Have you been to Germany or other countries before?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Assesses your travel history and visa compliance. Stating prior travels/visas honestly, and consistently explaining any previous visa refusals, is important.

Note

Concealing a prior visa refusal or an overstay can be risky; since such records are visible in the system, inconsistency causes loss of trust.

11

Will your family come with you, where are they?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Assesses your family plan. Knowing whether your family will join or stay in your country, and the family reunification process in general terms if joining, is helpful.

Note

Vague answers on whether your family will come can conflict with a later family-reunion application; keeping your plan clear is helpful.

12

What is your long-term plan?

What this question assesses / how to approach

Assesses the seriousness of your intention in Germany. A consistent, realistic account of your professional goals and plan to work/live in Germany builds trust.

Note

A long-term plan inconsistent with the work purpose (e.g. unrelated to the job) can conflict with the visa's purpose; alignment with your job offer matters.

General Tips

01

Be punctual, arrive on time for the interview.

02

Keep your documents and statements consistent with each other.

03

Speak honestly and clearly, without exaggeration.

04

Do not invent things you do not know; if unsure, say so.

05

Stay calm; the questions aim to understand your intention and preparation.
